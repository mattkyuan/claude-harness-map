Floors: where it comes from. Inner rings: in context. Drag to rotate, tap a node.
The model is a sharp temp, booked for one session. The agency sends them in with an onboarding binder, which is what this map shows. They keep no notes of their own: before every reply they reread that same binder plus the whole email thread so far, mostly from a cheap photocopy. When the session ends, so does the contract, and the next session gets a fresh temp with the same binder.
Inner ring: pages in the binder, reread before every reply. Next ring: the apps on their laptop, names only; the help page opens on first use. Outer: training guides and old notes pulled from the shared drive when a task needs them. Wiring: what IT and compliance enforce: laptop locks, approvals, automatic checks. It takes no room in the binder.
The temp never touches the systems directly: the company's tools (the harness) do every action and report back. Anything worth keeping has to go in the binder or the shared drive, because the temp will not be back.
